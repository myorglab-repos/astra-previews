Product definition / 01

Product purpose and configuration

Pause at a fully extended or turned pose:

80-second full-product study. Use fullscreen for the synchronized detail view. The camera orbits while only the shoulder carrier turns physically.

The product is a refillable, floor-mounted heavy bag with rear-mounted pneumatic arms. A concealed central column supports a rotating shoulder cartridge. The main bag and its nominal 200 lb fill remain stationary while the shoulder band and arm pair turn toward the opponent. Three camera positions on the crown support the proposed tracking system; a portable touchscreen provides control.

The current development configuration uses soft continuum arms: flexible pressure bladders, programmed textile restraint, elastic return bands, a soft torsion wrist and padded gloves. There are no metal links, pins, rigid elbow hinges, wrist cams, bearings or hard cuffs beyond the shoulder termination. Metal remains in the protected shoulder root, rotary cartridge, mast and base. Earlier cylinder models remain archived in their original folders.

Inflation is intended to extend and steer the arm. Venting removes pressure so stored elastic strain and the precurved textile structure can return it toward guard. A limp bladder cannot actively pull itself back. Reliable loaded recoil, gravity support and the desired punching speed still need physical development.

This is an editable development model and an explanatory assembly package. The films show all currently modeled parts, with an explicit connection register. They do not show every component required to manufacture a working machine: supplier internals, some fixings, seals, electronics and control hardware remain undefined. Animation is prescribed motion, not a simulation of pressure forces or a contact-safety test.

Product definition / 03

Base, column and load path

The steel column, floor flange and weld are delivered as one welded unit. They remain together in the installation animation. Four top-down concrete screw envelopes are shown separately. Actual anchor selection must account for concrete condition, slab thickness, reinforcement, edge distance, spacing, embedment and the installation procedure for a selected product. The generic screws in the model have no load rating.

The intended arm load path is glove → soft wrist harness → forearm textile/bladders → flexible inter-section saddle → upper textile/bladders → shoulder retention → rear carrier tube → arm flange → bolted yoke → rotor sleeve → bearing pair → fixed hub → column → welded floor flange → anchors → concrete. This chain is not yet physically complete: textile terminations, shoulder retention, bearing retention and hub clamping remain open. Bag-body impacts also pass through the fill, soft liners and their supports; they must not simply be assigned the arm load path.

For an illustrative static screening calculation, moment is M = F × perpendicular lever arm. The approximate bearing reaction couple associated with that moment is M / bearing spacing. These relationships do not establish fatigue, shock amplification, anchor tension distribution or contact safety. An engineering load model must include eccentric blows, simultaneous bag/arm loading, drive stalls, off-axis strikes and fatigue. Qualification needs instrumented prototype testing and structural analysis using agreed load cases.

Product definition / 04

Rotary cartridge and the arm fixing

The cartridge is mounted at shoulder height. The main bag and column stay stationary. Rotating only the arm carrier and shoulder band avoids intentionally accelerating the full 200 lb fill mass, but the rotating band still contributes inertia and must remain separated from the fixed fill and textiles.

J01 is a proposed rotor weldment: rotor sleeve, two saddle webs and rear yoke. The yoke is 368 × 80 × 12 mm. The webs intersect the sleeve wall while leaving the nominal inner bore clear. Weld beads depict the intended connections; they do not define weld throat, penetration, process, inspection or fatigue strength. Bearing seats would need a manufacturing strategy that controls weld distortion, including finish machining if appropriate.

J02 is the explicit removable arm interface. Each arm flange is 64 × 80 × 10 mm. Four 8.8 mm clearance holes sit on a 40 × 52 mm pattern. The corresponding yoke holes share those centers. Each hole receives a nominal M8 × 35 screw, washer and hex nut. Screw shafts have modeled 1.25 mm helical pitch and socket-head recesses. The 12 mm yoke, 10 mm flange and 1.6 mm washer give a 23.6 mm nominal grip. A 6.5 mm nut leaves approximately 4.9 mm nominal screw projection. Nut internal threads, thread class, chamfers, runout, bolt grade, preload, locking method and torque are not specified. No strength rating follows from these dimensions.

J03 is a hollow 26 × 3 mm carrier tube welded normal to the arm flange, with a bent route to the shoulder. The tube and flange are one delivered welded subassembly. Bend manufacturability, material specification and the tube-to-shoulder connection require further work. The current soft-arm proposal terminates at a rounded shoulder eye under a foam boot. A continuous textile harness loops through this eye; weld sizing, webbing contact pressure, seam schedule and retention strength remain unresolved (M01/M06).

The root animation separates the mating plates, nuts, washers and screws. It shows the final seated position and nominal screw advance. Tool-access volumes, counter-holding the nuts and installation access through the soft service opening are not verified. The animation is an explanatory sequence, not an approved installation procedure.

The bearing geometry includes separate inner/outer race envelopes and balls. It omits cages, seals, selected raceway geometry, shaft/housing fits, axial shoulders or retainers and preload. The fixed hub currently lacks a complete split-clamp design and fastening hardware. SKF's bearing-interface guidance is relevant to the next design step: bearing rings require appropriate axial location, rather than merely occupying the right space. SKF bearing interfaces.

Three chambers are the initial minimum symmetric layout for section extension and two-direction curvature control. Two serial sections create a virtual upper arm and forearm without a hard elbow. Opposed wrist channels add reversible twist. This count is an engineering proposal, not proof of controllability: chamber coupling, torsion, buckling, gravity and contact can reduce the usable degrees of freedom. Additional chambers should be justified by measured controllability rather than added for appearance.

Each main bladder has a soft closed end and an integral flexible inlet/pigtail. The main bladder wall is modeled; inlet-bore continuity and leak-tight bonds are not resolved. Wrist-cell geometry represents the external pressure envelope rather than a production wall/port definition. The sleeve fits around it and is registered to reinforced textile saddles. There are no metallic end clamps. The modeled pale bladder, charcoal sleeve and red bias region make the layers distinguishable. The red region is part of the programmed sleeve, not a separate motor or rigid strip. A replaceable foam/textile laminate covers the actuator bundle.

The knitting pattern must permit axial extension while controlling radial ballooning. Local stitch compliance and registered strain restrictions bias curvature; the three sleeves must be arranged so their combined differential strain spans the intended bending directions. The mesh texture is a construction illustration, not a machine-knitting file or tested yarn architecture. Exact yarn, gauge, loop topology, reinforcement transitions and tension remain prototype variables.

Inflation extends; venting permits recoil

The operating cycle is vented guard → controlled fill → extension with directional pressure bias → brief target pose → metered exhaust → elastic return. Balanced chamber response tends toward common extension. Unequal measured chamber strain biases the bending plane. Equal commanded pressure does not necessarily give equal strain because loads and textile construction differ.

Two elastic textile bands along each section store strain during extension. A separate continuous webbing harness retains the sections and glove even if a bladder loses pressure. The harness does not act as a rigid link. Precurvature and differential return strain bias the unpressurized rest shape toward guard; the model displays the intended shape. Whether it can hold the glove up against gravity without excessive preload is unproven. A completely vented soft arm may sag. Return force, creep, fatigue, heat and snapback must be measured.

If passive recoil proves too slow or inconsistent, first adjust mass, textile preload and exhaust restriction within a validated energy limit. Active antagonistic bending chambers are a possible later architecture and would change the chamber count. They are not secretly included in this model. Vacuum is not assumed to create a controlled return stroke.

The interactive channel diagram shows qualitative coordination, not valve timing or a pressure recipe. Air does not simply pass serially from U1 through the other chambers: each chamber needs an isolated branch so pressures can differ. Exhaust must also be independently controlled or constrained by a verified circuit.

The desired straight reach is approximately 858.8 mm shoulder-to-wrist; the glove contact point reaches the opponent centerline in the animation. The original outward timing target was 0.25 seconds; N1 slows the explanatory sequence fourfold, showing that interval over one second. Neither that timing nor the reach is demonstrated under pneumatic actuation or contact load. The virtual pose solver does not account for stretching energy, flow limits, tube interaction or continuum-arm equilibrium.

Soft wrist with no cam or bearing

T1 and T2 are opposite-handed textile-reinforced soft torsion concepts. Their chamber centerlines occupy different radii so the two helices do not cross in the nominal layout. A soft shear core and sewn proximal/distal textile cuffs carry and retain the glove; there is no central metal shaft, hard guide or rigid end ring. The cover is removed in the wrist film.

Selective inflation is intended to twist one way; the opposite chamber reverses that bias. The glove orientations are prescribed targets. A helical-looking tube alone is not a demonstrated torsion actuator. Fiber constraints, end terminations, radial growth, coupled extension/bending, rotation under glove load and available torque need a separate wrist prototype. The required palm range must be established before selecting geometry. Soft construction also does not eliminate stored pneumatic energy or impact hazard.

What the research supports

MIT CSAIL's PneuAct combines silicone tubes with knitted sleeves whose stitch patterns program bending. Its design preview is quasi-static; the published construction does not demonstrate this two-section punching arm or its speed. We apply its textile-guidance principle as a research reference. PneuAct project · CHI 2022 paper.

The linked MIT Lincoln Laboratory SPROUT robot is an airtight fabric tube that unfurls from a fixed base. Air inflates it and a motor controls deployment. It supports the idea of compliant, pressure-steered structures, but does not establish passive recoil, repeated striking or contact safety for this product. The present arm expands and bends in place; it is not an everting vine mechanism. MIT Lincoln Laboratory: SPROUT.

The Soft Robotics Toolkit describes how strain-limiting layers and fiber orientations can produce extension, bending and twisting. Our opposed wrist layout and multi-chamber coordination remain proposed applications of these principles. Fiber-reinforced actuator motion.

Product definition / 06

Bag construction, refill and surface materials

The soft body surrounds an annular refill volume and central sleeve, allowing fill around the column. The proposed fill is rubber chips or short rubber filament, not a rigid annular insert. The rendered fill volume is a placeholder for loose material. Use clean, characterized sample material and measure bulk density, settling and compression before setting the fill recipe. Fill mass is bulk density × usable filled volume; subtract seams, partitions, mechanism clearances and liners rather than using the outside cylinder volume.

The model separates an outer coated-fabric cover, comfort foam, fill region, central sleeve/core padding, lower support boot, upper closure, seams and partition elements. The rotating shoulder band requires its own retained fill compartment and protected interfaces to the fixed upper and lower bag. Leakage, abrasion and entanglement at these boundaries remain unresolved. Flexible overlap geometry alone does not prove pinch protection.

Product definition / 07

Vision, handheld and pneumatic control architecture

Three camera positions sit around the fixed crown at approximately 120° spacing. Recessed windows and a soft crown guard are modeled. Image sensors, optics, boards, internal retaining features and wiring are not selected. Calibrate overlapping fields of view and test self-occlusion before claiming continuous opponent tracking.

The intended control chain is camera images → opponent estimate with confidence → desired relative bearing → bounded shoulder-carrier motion. Only the shoulder carrier and its padded band turn; the 200 lb main fill mass remains stationary. An encoder is needed on the carrier. Continuous rotation additionally needs a selected rotary pneumatic union and electrical transfer; otherwise specify and enforce a finite rotation range. The current union and drive are envelopes with unresolved mounting and internal details.

The portable handheld retains a touchscreen-only front, recessed side power key, rear service-panel screws and charge-port reservation. It is a form study with mock screen graphics. Display, battery, radio, thermal management, sealing and drop performance require development. The side power key is not a safety-rated emergency stop.

Air source and isolated branches

Keep the heavy compressor, treatment equipment and any selected reservoir at a fixed base service location or remote plant connection. They are system requirements, not detailed supplier components in this Blender file. Protected shoulder/rear manifolds are represented as purchased envelopes. They feed eight flexible branches per arm: U1–U3, F1–F3, T1–T2. Metal valves and hard fittings remain behind shoulder padding, outside the distal contact structure.

A circuit still needs inlet filtration, regulated supply, pressure relief, monitored supply isolation, branch fill/exhaust control, pressure sensing and a defined fault vent path. A pressure-only controller is insufficient to establish arm pose; add calibrated soft strain/shape sensing and external tracking during development. Sensor hardware and routing are not yet modeled. Valves, regulators, rotary union and hose ports have not been sized or assigned working pressure.

Air mass flow changes chamber pressure and volume. Pressure then interacts with textile strain, elastic return, gravity and contact loads. Source pressure alone does not set punch speed. Identify pressure/volume curves and inlet/exhaust conductance under representative loads; then size the full supply path and assess recovery between cycles. Account for compressed-air demand, reservoir depletion, regulator droop, heat, exhaust noise and hose resistance.

Control states should include disabled/vented, enabled guard, controlled fill/steer, return, and fault. Low tracking confidence, communication loss, a chamber pressure fault, loss of power and torn textile each need a defined bounded response. Simply opening every exhaust may drop an arm; uncontrolled snapback is also undesirable. The response must be characterized and supported by an independent motion-enable/stopping architecture. No functioning controller is delivered here.

Product definition / 09

Engineering handoff and release gates

Is the soft-arm design fragile?

It is a potentially vulnerable prototype, not a proven durable punching arm. Compliance can reduce exposed hard contact surfaces, but it does not establish puncture resistance, seam strength, fatigue life or safe impact energy. Pressurization also stiffens the arm and stores energy. No allowable strike force, impact count, operating pressure or service interval has been verified for this geometry.

The priorities below are engineering recommendations; the N1 update changes presentation and explanatory animation, not the unqualified M1 material stack.

PneuAct reported stable behavior over 100 inflation/deflation cycles for its tested actuators. That limited pneumatic cycling experiment is not evidence of repeated boxing-impact durability for these larger arms. The proposed multi-section arm and soft torsion wrist require their own tests. PneuAct, CHI 2022, section 5.1.

Establish intended strike impulse, peak load, off-axis load, contact energy and lifetime duty cycle before setting pass/fail criteria. Use instrumented fixtures to compare force, 3D shape, leak rate, seam damage and permanent deformation before and after pressure cycling and impacts. The Soft Robotics Toolkit describes force/torque and 3D motion measurements useful for actuator characterization; those measurements do not by themselves qualify human contact. Actuator testing.

The development package supplies editable Blender geometry, exploded/assembly animation scenes, a product walkthrough, object-level connection register, nominal root geometry checks and animation-coverage checks. Blender meshes are not a substitute for toleranced mechanical CAD, supplier models or manufacturing drawings.

Before procurement or fabrication release, complete: dimensional drawings and tolerance stack-ups; genuine purchased component selections and datasheets; material specifications; weld and finish schedules; fastener specifications, locking and torque; bearings and retention; pneumatic schematic and pressure ratings; electrical drawings and wiring; software/state-machine definitions; service access and assembly tooling; inspection and acceptance criteria.

Before user-contact trials, establish and test structural loads, repeated-impact endurance, padding displacement, pneumatic faults, loss of power, loss of tracking, joint retention, pinch exposure and the independent stopping/enable architecture. The current package cannot establish that this machine safely withstands significant strikes. That conclusion requires the completed engineering and test evidence.

For the soft arm, begin with one sleeved bladder and one three-chamber section. Measure pressure/strain, curvature, loaded extension, hysteresis, fatigue and vented recovery. Then characterize the wrist separately and a two-section module on a guarded instrumented fixture. Establish maximum contact energy and stopping behavior before any person-facing trials. Test damaged bladders, hose rupture, cover displacement, glove pull-off, gravity sag and elastic-return failure. The current model has no verified impact-force rating.
