# D. Continuum pneumatic intent

Eight isolated branches per arm: manifold → U1/U2/U3, F1/F2/F3, T1/T2. No serial cell-to-cell plumbing. Exposed distal elements remain soft bladder/textile/foam; strain limiting and passive textile return are inherited concepts, not material-qualified components.

| Strike | More emphasized chambers | Prescribed direction | Palm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straight | U1, F1, F3, T1 | Forward extension | Down |
| Hook | U2, F1, T2 | Lateral bend | Inward |
| Uppercut | U3, F2, T2 | Rising bend | Up |
| Body | U1, U2, F3, T1 | Forward bend plus downward root pitch | Down |


Profiles in [airflow_profiles.json](airflow_profiles.json) are arbitrary visual emphasis values. The illustrative scene modifies left-arm chamber widths only for the four review chapters. Less-emphasized cells receive less fill rather than the old vent-during-fill cue; all active cells vent and relax during recovery. Cyan/orange/gold retain fill/vent/return meanings. The mapping expresses an engineering hypothesis, not a physical valve recipe.

Track B must identify pressure, cell strain, curvature, tip motion, hysteresis and repeatability on actual coupons. Track C must measure contact on an instrumented fixture and examine faults. No numerical requirements or results are invented here. B-03, B-06 and C-01 remain open.

See [AIRFLOW_PEDAGOGY](AIRFLOW_PEDAGOGY.html) for the exact drawing rule and its limits.

**Track A does not prove strike impulse.** These are prescribed geometry and illustrative pneumatic cues. Measured propulsion, contact force, durability and real CV remain unverified.
